🌌 Beyond the Solar System

Deep Space

Nebulae, galaxies and the farthest reaches of the universe, captured by NASA’s most powerful telescopes.

🌟 Star birth

Carina Nebula — “Cosmic Cliffs”

Webb’s infrared view of the Carina Nebula reveals areas of star birth that were hidden before. About 7,600 light-years away, this landscape of gas and dust is the edge of a giant star-forming region, and its tallest “peaks” are roughly 7 light-years high.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

🌌 Galaxy interaction

Stephan’s Quintet

A visual grouping of five galaxies, photographed by Webb in great detail. Four of them are interacting in a cosmic dance that may eventually merge them. The mosaic holds over 150 million pixels and was built from nearly 1,000 image files.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

🔭 Distant universe

Webb’s First Deep Field

The deepest, sharpest infrared image of the distant universe taken at its release. The patch of sky is about the size of a grain of sand held at arm’s length and holds thousands of galaxies, some of them seen as they were over 13 billion years ago.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

💫 Stellar death

Southern Ring Nebula

A planetary nebula about 2,500 light-years away, imaged by Webb’s NIRCam and MIRI instruments. A dying star sheds its outer layers in rings of gas and dust, giving a glimpse of how our own Sun will end its life billions of years from now.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

🛰️ Observatory

Hubble Space Telescope

Launched in 1990, Hubble has made over 1.5 million observations. It helped pin down the age of the universe and captured iconic images such as the Pillars of Creation.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI
